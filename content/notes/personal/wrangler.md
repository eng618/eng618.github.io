## ✅ Before each trip / monthly

- [ ] Check engine oil level and look for leaks.
- [ ] Check coolant level in overflow bottle.
- [ ] Check brake fluid level and look for wet spots at calipers/lines.
- [ ] Check power steering fluid level (if equipped with hydraulic steering).
- [ ] Check windshield washer fluid level.
- [ ] Inspect tires: pressure, tread depth, sidewall cuts, uneven wear.
- [ ] Quick underbody look: skid plates, control arms, shocks, exhaust hangers.
- [ ] Check exterior lights (headlights, brake, turn, reverse) and wipers.

---

## 🔧 Every 7,500–10,000 miles or 12 months

(Jeep uses an oil life monitor; don’t exceed 10k/12 months even if it hasn’t lit up.)

- [ ] Change engine oil and filter (full synthetic) – 3.6L Pentastar.
- [ ] Rotate tires (front ↔ rear, follow pattern for your tire type).
- [ ] Inspect brake pads, rotors, hoses, parking brake operation.
- [ ] Inspect battery: terminals clean/tight, case not swollen, hold‑down tight.
- [ ] Inspect engine air filter (replace early if you off‑road in dust).
- [ ] Inspect exhaust system for leaks and loose hangers.
- [ ] Inspect cooling system: hoses, radiator, look for leaks, coolant level/condition.
- [ ] General suspension/steering check: ball joints, tie rods, track bar, bushings, shocks.

---

## 🧰 Every 20,000 miles

- [ ] Replace cabin / A/C filter.
- [ ] Inspect front and rear suspension and tie rod ends more carefully (play, torn boots).
- [ ] Inspect CV/u‑joints and driveshafts.
- [ ] Inspect brake linings, parking brake mechanism, adjust parking brake if needed.
- [ ] Check front and rear axle fluid levels.

---

## 🔩 Every 30,000–40,000 miles

- [ ] Replace engine air cleaner filter (at 30k if not done earlier).
- [ ] Replace/inspect transfer case fluid (many dealers call this at 30k; Jeep often lists 60k—pick a value based on how much you off‑road).
- [ ] Change front & rear axle gear oil around 40k (earlier if lots of water crossings / towing).
- [ ] If manual transmission and used for towing/off‑road, change manual transmission fluid around this point.

---

## 🔧 60,000 miles

- [ ] Replace spark plugs (3.6L typically at 100k in the book, but many shops do 60–100k; at minimum plan on 100k).
- [ ] Flush and replace engine coolant (Jeep also calls for 10 years/150k max, but 60–100k is a conservative window).
- [ ] Inspect serpentine belt for cracks/glazing; replace if any doubt.
- [ ] Consider automatic transmission fluid service if not “lifetime” or if you tow/off‑road frequently.

---

## 🔩 100,000 miles

- [ ] Replace spark plugs on the 3.6L if not already done.
- [ ] Coolant flush if not done earlier.
- [ ] Inspect/replace accessory drive (serpentine) belt, tensioner, and pulleys.
- [ ] Inspect/replace PCV valve as needed.
- [ ] Full inspection: suspension joints, bushings, steering components, hoses, and lines.

---

## ⏱ Time-based items (even if miles are low)

- [ ] Brake fluid flush: every ~3 years. (Common best practice even if not heavily stressed in the basic schedule.)
- [ ] Engine coolant: 10 years/150k miles max per Jeep, but you can choose 7–10 years.
- [ ] Accessory belt: inspect annually after ~60k miles or 5–7 years; replace if cracked/noisy.
- [ ] Wiper blades: about yearly or as soon as they streak.

---

## 🧰 Simple “garage check” routine

Do this a couple of times a year or after hard off‑road trips:

- [ ] Torque check on lug nuts and key suspension bolts (control arms, track bar, skid plates) according to spec.
- [ ] Look for rust on frame, brake lines, and body mounts; clean and treat as needed.
- [ ] Inspect recovery points, hitch, and tow hooks.
- [ ] Check for play in steering wheel and any clunks when turning or hitting bumps.
